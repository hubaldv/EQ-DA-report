\section*{Introduction}
This short report describes the pressure dynamics of a Quattroflow \textcolor{red}{xxx} pump, both without and with an Equilibar backpressure regulator installed downstream of the pump. The Quattroflow pump is operated at different speeds ranging from 250 to 1000 RPM. As the roller pump contains 4 diaphragm pumps that are sequential driven by an excentric shaft, 4 pressure pulses are expected per revolution of the pump. As the experiments are performed without and with an Equilibar backpressure regulator installed downstream of the pump, it is expected that the Equilibar will significantly reduce the pressure fluctuations upstream of the regulator generated by the pump.

\section{Method}
To model a customer proces, a needle valve (Fitok MH6S-FNS4-V) is installed downstream of the proces to create a certain backpressure. For the first part, no Equilibar is installed, and both pressure sensors are connected directly to the outlet of the pump (in serie or parallel). For the second part, an Equilibar is installed downstream of the pump, and a different backpressures (0.25 to 2.5 bar) and pump rotations (250, 500, 750 and 1000 RPM) are applied. Pressure sensors are placed up- and downstream of the Equilibar. Figures \ref{fig:setup_with_equilibar} shows the setup with Equilibar. \\
During the experiments, two Keller 20SX sensors are used and connected to a PCS EQ-DA PCB for data acquisition. 
Each sensor is sampled at 1250 Hz and filtered with a digital biquad filter with a cutoff frequency of 1000 Hz. All collected data, including MCU timestamps, is communicated to a custom-designed GUI for visualization and logging to a \texttt{.csv} file. Further analysis is performed using \texttt{Python}, where a small portion of the signals (0.2 seconds) is shown on the x-axis throughout this report. As different absolut values might be large compared to the relative fluctuations, in some figures the average of the signals is subtracted. These pressures are called \textit{pressure fluctuation} $p'(t)$ and are determined by
\begin{equation}
    p'(t) = p(t) - \bar{p},
\end{equation}
where $p(t)$ is the absolute pressure signal and $\bar{p}$ is the average pressure over the shown time interval.

\section{Results without Equilibar}
For the first experiment, both sensors are connected directly to the outflow of the Quattroflow pump as shown in Figure \ref{fig:setup_1}. 
\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{images/no_equilibar.png}
    \caption{An EQ-DA is used to read out the two Keller 20SX sensors.}
    \label{fig:setup_1}
\end{figure}

Several tests are performed at different pump speeds: 250, 500, 750, and 1000 RPM. This results in approximately the following expected pulsing frequencies: 17, 33, 50, and 67 Hz.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{images/without_equilibar.eps}
    \caption{Pressure dynamics without an Equilibar.}
    \label{fig:without_equilibar_results}
\end{figure}

From Figure \ref{fig:without_equilibar_results}, it can be seen that at lower RPMs (250 and 500 RPM) both pressure curves look similar, except for some absolute pressure differences. This can be due to the fact that the sensors were not zeroed before the experiment. Additionally, a phase shift can be observed, which can be explained by the physical distance between both sensors. At higher RPMs (750 and 1000 RPM), the absolute pressure increases slightly, and the amplitude of the pressure fluctuations increases on the upstream side. The downstream side shows smaller pressure fluctuations at these higher frequencies. This can be explained by the damping effect of the differential pressure across the first sensor or some low-pass filtering within the sensor itself. It would be interesting to interchange the sensors to determine whether the effect is sensor-related or location-related; unfortunately, this was not performed during this experiment.

\section{Results with Equilibar and constant backpressure}
An Equilibar is connected to the outlet of the pump. The first pressure sensor is installed directly at the outlet of the pump (upstream side of the Equilibar) and the second pressure sensor is installed downstream of the Equilibar (downstream side). An EQ-DA is used to control the reference pressure at the Equilibar. The design of the Equilibar results in an upstream pressure that approximately equals the applied reference pressure. During these experiments, a constant reference pressure of 1 bar is applied at the Equilibar. Subsequently, the pump is again operated at different speeds: 250, 500, 750, and 1000 RPM.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{images/with_equilibar.eps}
    \caption{Pressure dynamics with an Equilibar.}
    \label{fig:with_equilibar_results}
\end{figure}

In Figure \ref{fig:with_equilibar_results}, the pressure dynamics with an Equilibar are shown. The top graphs show the absolute pressure over time for both sensors at different pump speeds. The bottom graphs show an average-subtracted pressure signal to better visualize the (relative) pressure fluctuations. \\
From this figure, it can be seen that the upstream pressure (sensor 1) again shows the expected pressure fluctuations, which increase in amplitude with increasing pump speed. However, the downstream pressure (sensor 2) now shows a more stable pressure with minimal fluctuations. This indicates that the Equilibar is effective in damping the downstream pressure fluctuations generated by the Quattroflow pump, even at higher pump speeds (peak-to-peak 10 mbar vs 30 mbar). However, the upstream pressure fluctuations are not damped and are slightly larger compared to the results without an Equilibar (peak-to-peak 80 mbar vs 60 mbar). In Section \ref{sec:conclusion}, some possible explanations are given for this observation. \\
It is important to note that some damping was observed in the `without Equilibar' experiment as well, especially at higher pump speeds. This could be related to the sensor response time, filtering within the sensor or the damping effect of the fluidic system itself. Observed damping might therefore not only be an effect of the Equilibar but also of the fluidic system and/or sensors used.

The frequency content of the pressure signals provides more insight into the pressure dynamics. In Figure \ref{fig:with_equilibar_freq_results}, this is shown for both sensors at different pump speeds. The expected pulsing frequencies (17, 33, 50, and 67 Hz) are again clearly visible in both sensors. This analysis can be used to determine the damping effect.

\begin{figure}[h!]

    \caption{Frequency representation of pressure dynamics with an Equilibar.}
    \label{fig:with_equilibar_freq_results}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Comparing the without and with Equilibar cases}
To compare the results of the `without Equilibar' and `with Equilibar' cases, the frequency representation of both experiments at 1000 RPM is shown in Figure \ref{fig:with_without_comparison_results}. It can be seen that the upstream pressure fluctuations are slightly increased when using the Equilibar compared to the setup without the Equilibar. A possible explanation for this observation is that the Equilibar introduces some additional flow resistance in the system, which could lead to increased pressure fluctuations upstream. This could be explained if the dynamics of the Equilibar are too slow to respond to the fast pressure pulses generated by the Quattroflow pump. Section \ref{sec:conclusion} elaborates a little bit further on this.\\

\section{Results with Equilibar and variable backpressures}
In this experiment, the Equilibar is again connected to the outlet of the pump with the same sensor configuration as in the previous section. However, the reference pressure at the Equilibar is varied between 0.25, 0.5, and 1 bar. The pump is operated constantly at a speed of 1000 RPM.

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{images/with_equilibar_variable.eps}
    \caption{Pressure dynamics with an Equilibar at variable backpressures.}
    \label{fig:with_equilibar_variable_results}
\end{figure}

During analysis, the absolute pressure is plotted as well as a relative pressure signal with the average subtracted. From Figure \ref{fig:with_equilibar_variable_results}, it can be seen that the upstream pressure fluctuations increase slightly with increasing backpressure. This can be explained by the fact that at higher backpressure, the Quattroflow pump has to work against a higher load, resulting in slightly larger pressure fluctuations. However, the downstream pressure remains stable with smaller fluctuations, regardless of the applied backpressure. \\
Again, some of the damping effect could be related to the fluidic system and/or sensors used, as was also observed in the previous section.

\newpage
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
It was expected that the Equilibar would significantly reduce the pressure fluctuations upstream of the regulator that are generated by the pump. However, the results show that the upstream pressure fluctuations are slightly increased when using the Equilibar compared to the setup without the Equilibar. A possible explanation for this observation is that the Equilibar introduces some additional flow resistance in the system, which could lead to increased pressure fluctuations upstream. This could be explained if the dynamics of the Equilibar are too slow to respond to the fast pressure pulses generated by the Quattroflow pump. 

\bigskip

Based on the results and observations from this report, it can be concluded that the Equilibar is effective in damping the pressure fluctuations downstream of the pump, providing a more stable pressure output (and flow output). However, further investigation is needed to fully understand the impact of the Equilibar on upstream pressure dynamics. 

\bigskip

Recommendations for further investigation:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Interchange the sensors to determine if the (damping) effect is sensor-related or location-related;
    \item Ensure no air bubbles are present in the fluidic system, as they could potentially influence the pressure readouts;
    \item Use a faster-responding Equilibar model to determine if the dynamics of the Equilibar are too slow to respond to the fast pressure pulses generated by the Quattroflow pump. Changing the diaphragm material could potentially help achieve this;
    \item Actively reduce fluctuations by closed-loop controlling the reference pressure at the Equilibar based on the fluctuating upstream pressure sensor signal. The downstream pressure could also potentially be controlled to maintain a even more constant pressure. The latter could also be used to create a (more) constant output flow.
\end{itemize}
